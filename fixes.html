When it goes wrong

Smudges, bubbles, and chips, and how I fix them without starting over.

Jump to a fix

  • A smudge on wet polish
  • Tiny bubbles
  • A chip at the tip
  • Polish on my skin
  • Streaky color

My fix kit

  • Polish remover
  • Cotton swabs
  • An old, small makeup brush
  • A nail file
  • Top coat

The one rule

Most of my mistakes used to turn into bigger mistakes because I panicked and tried to fix them right away. Now I stop, look at whether the polish is still wet or already dry, and pick the fix from there. Wet polish can usually be smoothed out. Dry polish needs a thin new layer on top.

The other thing I learned is that a fixed nail does not have to be perfect. If you went with one of the forgiving designs, a small patch blends right in.

Five problems I run into the most

A smudge on wet polish

What happened: I touched something before the coat dried, which happens to me pretty much every time.

How I fix it: I dab a fingertip in a tiny bit of remover and gently pat the dent until it smooths out, then add one thin coat over it.

Tiny bubbles

What happened: I shook the bottle, or put on one thick coat instead of two thin ones.

How I fix it: If it is still wet, I brush lightly over it once. If it dried, I wait, then add a thin coat. Next time I roll the bottle between my hands instead of shaking it.

A chip at the tip

What happened: Usually dishes, or I skipped the top coat on the very edge of the nail.

How I fix it: I file the rough edge smooth, dab color into just the chipped spot, let it dry, and put top coat over the whole nail.

Polish on my skin

What happened: Too much polish on the brush, so it ran into the edges of the nail.

How I fix it: I dip a small brush or a cotton swab in remover and trace around the nail while the polish is still wet. It comes off much easier than it does after it dries.

Streaky color

What happened: Light and sheer colors almost always look streaky after one coat, and old polish gets thick and drags.

How I fix it: I add a second thin coat and that usually evens it out. If the bottle has gone gloopy, I replace it instead of fighting it.

When I just start over

If more than two or three nails are messed up, I take it all off and start again. Fixing that many nails one by one takes longer than a fresh coat, and it never looks as smooth.
